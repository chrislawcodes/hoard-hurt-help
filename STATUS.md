## Recently Shipped

- **Headhunter loses its escape hatches — the hedges were doing the deciding** (2026-08-23, branch `headhunter-aggressive`, Direct Path) — the v10 preset told it to hunt helpers but also gave it two ways out (*"when nobody is worth hitting, HOARD"* and *"never swing at a player you expect to swing at you"*). Measured across M_7180 and M_7186 it attacked **26% and 34%** while HELPing more than half the time. Both hedges read as sensible and both were where the decision actually happened: **given any way to opt out, the model took it.** **There is no honest reason to opt out** — a HELPer was available on **69 of 69 turns** across those matches, and at v10 hitting one pays **6, exactly what a pact pays**. So a pure attacker earns the same per turn as a pure cooperator *and* damages someone; what it gives up is help from others, which a known attacker was never going to receive. It now reads: HURT every turn, aim at whoever is HELPing you first, HOARD only if nobody is helping anybody, **never HELP**. That makes it the roster's only pure predator — it asks nobody for anything and its whole score is what it takes. The test now pins `Never HELP` so the hedge cannot creep back, with the measured reason beside it. **Context for why this matters beyond one seat:** across both v10 matches the field minus Headhunter attacked at **7.1% and 6.5%** — inside the pre-v10 range — so raising the helper tier made an aggressive preset *viable* without changing anyone else's behaviour. If the goal is a higher field-wide attack rate, more attack-shaped seats is the lever that works; the tier itself cannot go past 6, where the cliff sits. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1918 tests**.

- **Hitting a helper now pays 6, and Headhunter replaces Loyal Partner — rules v10** (2026-08-23, branch `headhunter-tier6`, Direct Path) — **why:** ten measured matches put the attack rate at 3.6-8.6%, and the ordering shows why. With nobody helping you, taking the pot alone pays 8 and a pact pays 6, so an attack at 5 was your **third-best move** and agents rationally skipped it. At 6 it ties the pact and stops being dominated. **This deliberately reverses a v9 decision**: the tier was held strictly BELOW a pact so a head-to-head endgame kept a real decision in it (at parity, attacking a cooperator pays what cooperating pays *and* damages them, so there is no reason not to swing). That reasoning is still true — it is no longer the priority, and both the constant's comment and the guard test now say so rather than quietly dropping it. **Seven remains forbidden and the guard still blocks it**, mutation-verified: simulation puts the cliff exactly there, where an attack beats a pact outright, cooperation falls by a third and winning scores drop from ~15 to ~11. **Headhunter** is the seat that exercises the change: it is the only preset that picks its target by what a THIRD PARTY is doing, where every other attacker hits its own partner (Turncoat, Sandbagger) or hits back (Tit-for-Tat, Champion). **Its fallback is the POT, not a pact** — falling back to a mutual HELP would have made it a cooperator that occasionally swings, which three seats already cover; hoarding when there is no target means it never asks anyone for anything, so expect a high HOARD share beside the high HURT share. **The swap cost the pacifist control** — Loyal Partner was the only preset at 0% HURT across ten matches, the seat that told us whether the retaliation rules elsewhere were doing any work. That loss is recorded in the module docstring and the test, so if the question "does retaliation matter" comes back, the control has to be rebuilt first. Note `loyal_partner` also exists as a SCRIPTED BOT strategy in `app/engine/bots/` — a separate system, deliberately untouched. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1918 tests**.

- **"No Playbook" preset added — the control the roster never had** (2026-08-21, branch `preset-no-playbook`, Direct Path) — every preset so far asserts that a human-written strategy is worth having, and **nothing in six measured matches has ever tested that assertion**. No Playbook is handed no plan at all and told to derive one from the rules it already receives, so it measures the seven authored strategies against whatever the model works out on its own. **It is a control, not another strategy** — it is handed no plan and asked to derive one, which is a different job from the seven that carry a plan. The same change also **deletes the docstring's "seven is the practical ceiling" claim**, which was wrong and load-bearing in the wrong direction: a ceiling argues against adding anything, when the real bar is only whether a preset behaves differently from the ones already there. The distinctness test is that bar and it stays. Kingslayer's retirement never depended on the ceiling anyway — its rule could not execute, because a profitable HURT needs the victim to be HELPing you and the leader is the player least likely to. **It must never name a move**: a test asserts the words HOARD, HELP and HURT do not appear in it, mutation-verified by slipping "consider a HELP pact" into the body and watching the guard fire. The moment it suggests a move it stops being a control and becomes a weak eighth strategy. **It also has to be legible or a win teaches nothing**, so it is told to state its current plan in its thinking every turn and say when and why it changes — the thinking field is recorded and surfaces in the replay, so what it invents can be read rather than guessed at. Context for why this matters now: across M_6915 and M_6918 the winners were the presets that do NOT throw the first punch (Loyal Partner 4.25/7, then Tit-for-Tat 4.83/7), while all three proactive-attack presets finished on zero in both — including two byte-identical Sandbagger twins at 0.33 and 0.00. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1918 tests**.

- **v9 measured over three matches; the tiered HURT works, the tie claim was half what one match suggested** (2026-08-20, branch `match-runbook-pattern`, Direct Path) — [#709](https://github.com/chrislawcodes/hoard-hurt-help/pull/709) made a HURT pay off what the TARGET is doing. Three real matches on the v9 presets: **M_6817, M_6855, M_6879**. **What replicated: attacking is alive.** HURT ran 5.7% / 4.2% / 4.6% against v8's **1.4%** — three to four times the rate in every run, with all four tiers in use. Across the three, **13 of 39 attacks landed at the +5 or +3 tiers**, which paid literally nothing before v9, and the block fired 6 times as mutual pairs that nobody was told about. **What did NOT replicate: the tie rate.** 14% / 33% / 29%, averaging ~25% against v8's 43%. The first match was the outlier — v9 roughly halved ties rather than cutting them by two thirds, and writing down 14% after one run would have been wrong. **The operational half cost more than the design half.** A retry storm was found and fixed ([#713](https://github.com/chrislawcodes/hoard-hurt-help/pull/713)): the subscription wall says *"You've hit your session limit"*, the backoff grepped for *"usage limit"*, and one agent logged **3,873 launches to play 24 turns** — roughly 30,000 rejected calls per match, and the quota it burned stalled M_6855's final round. The fix keys off the SHAPE of a failure (non-zero exit inside 15s) rather than its wording, because the wording is what failed. M_6879 then ran **39 launches, zero backoffs, 280/280 moves and talk**. **Also settled: MCP tools are now deferred** behind ToolSearch and `claude-haiku-4-5` is not on the exemption list, so haiku silently stopped being able to play mid-session with no local change at all — fixed by putting ToolSearch first in `--allowedTools` and telling the prompt to load the tools, which keeps the cheap model. **The whole procedure is now written down** in `scripts/match_runner/README.md` with the traps that cost each run: seats join *held* and are deleted at start unless the loops are already live; `pgrep -fc` under-reports and caused a healthy loop set to be "restarted" into four supervisors per agent; the spectator endpoint needs its `/state` suffix or it 404s silently; and a monitor must poll every 15 minutes reporting only end/backoff/stall, because a per-turn monitor wakes the agent ~20 times a match and costs more than the match. `analyse_match.py` produces the standard report and leads with validity, since a match where nobody could answer still completes and looks normal. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1918 tests**.

It now strikes whoever is HELPing it, from halfway through the match, in a round's last two turns — no consent required. *Hoarder* was told to abandon the pot and take a pact whenever others crowded in, i.e. to fold at exactly the moment staying would thin everyone else's share; it now attacks one of the cooperators it failed to keep paired off. **Underdog's Champion was a near-duplicate of Tit-for-Tat** — same one-partner loop, same leave-if-unpaid rule, and a word-for-word identical retaliation line — separated only by a recruitment filter. It now also refuses to HELP anyone in the top half for the whole match, which is a behavioural difference rather than a targeting preference, and keeps the anti-runaway role in the roster. **Three presets needed nothing:** Loyal Partner and Dealmaker never attack, so v9 passes them by, and Turncoat's ban on hitting non-helpers survives — its *reason* expired (the take is no longer zero) but the behaviour is still right, because the con pays 6.4-8.0 a turn against 5.0 for taking the small takes, and attacking openly is what stops anyone pacting with it. **A correction worth recording:** the first version of that comparison scored Turncoat's partnerless turns as zero when its own rule says to HOARD on them, which made the margin look like 0.2 points a turn instead of 1.4-3.0. Presets stay bare instructions — no reasoning in the prompt text; the rules text owns the numbers. **Open and deliberately unconstrained:** Sandbagger has no limit on how many times it strikes in the second half, so it may converge on Turncoat-with-a-slow-start. - **HURT now pays the attacker off what the TARGET was doing — rules v9** (2026-08-19, branch `tiered-hurt-v9`, Direct Path) — **the problem, measured in M_6809:** a player with no partner and no share of the pot had literally nothing to do, because HURT paid the attacker zero unless the target happened to be HELPing them. Sandbagger narrated its own defeat in the talk phase (*"pacts lock 30 and claim the round-win split. Game over."*) and cooperated into last place, because the board offered no alternative. Attacks ran at **1.4% of 280 moves**, and 3 of 7 rounds tied — two of them at exactly 30, the pure-cooperation ceiling. **The change:** a HURT still costs the target `HURT_POINTS`, but what the attacker *gains* now scales with what the target had on the table — betraying a helper **+18** (unchanged), hitting someone who is HELPing another **+5**, hitting a HOARDer **+3**, hitting someone who is themselves HURTing **0**, and two players who HURT each other **block** — both swing, both miss, no damage and no take either way. `hurt_take()` is the single source (resolver, viewer mirror, replay legend, per-move chip and the agent-facing rules text all call it), with `hurt_blocks()` beside it because applying one without the other would pay a take on an attack that never landed. **Two design calls that the first implementation got wrong and the tests caught:** a betrayal bonus is **never split** between attackers — it is earned from a relationship, not grabbed off the table, so a third party piling onto the same victim must not be able to halve it — and a betrayer is **excluded from the split count**, or a betrayal would quietly thin what the other attackers share. **`HURT_TAKE_HELPER` is deliberately BELOW a pact (5 against 6):** at parity, attacking a cooperator pays exactly what cooperating pays *and* damages them, so in a head-to-head endgame there is no reason not to swing and the final turn stops being a decision. That relationship, plus betrayal > helper > hoarder > 0, is now pinned by `test_the_hurt_tiers_keep_their_design_order` with the failure mode for each written down. **Modelled before building:** the tiers behave as a dial (attacks 22.7% → 31.2% in simulation) with a cliff at 8/6 where cooperation collapses to 20% and winning scores halve. **Stated limitation:** that simulation attacks at 22.7% where the real match measured 1.4%, so it never reproduces the tie problem and cannot say whether this fixes it — the case for shipping is that a losing player finally has a move, not a predicted tie rate. Three stale display paths fixed in passing: the score-floor note still said a HURT on a zeroed player "costs the attacker their turn" (the attacker is now paid for the swing regardless), and the mirror and legend both priced HURT the old way. Mutation-verified both directions: changing a tier value leaves the suite green (it is genuinely one number to tune), while making the betrayal bonus split fails two tests. `RULES_VERSION` v8 → v9. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1918 tests**.

- **All seven presets rewritten for the v8 payoffs, Kingslayer retired, Buzzer-Beater rebuilt as Turncoat** (2026-08-19, branch `presets-v8`, Direct Path) — [#705](https://github.com/chrislawcodes/hoard-hurt-help/pull/705) moved the pot to 8 and the betrayal bonus to 14 without touching the presets, which were written for a game where hoarding beat stabbing. Each was audited against the new arithmetic one at a time. **Three findings drove most of it.** (1) **Calendar timing was measured meaningless** — a HURT does identical damage on every turn but the first, because a round score is only clipped at zero, so "never HURT before a round's last turn" bought nothing and cost every earlier chance to strike; presets now time a strike off the victim's score ("as soon as the hit will not drop them to zero"). (2) **Dealmaker's signature move was a bad deal for both sides** — its take-turns trade misses the mutual bonus entirely and pays 2 a turn against a straight pact's 6, which is why nobody ever took it; the swap is now same-turn. (3) **Retaliation had to be rebuilt, not removed** — the shipped "HURT them next round" aimed at a score that had already been counted and paid the retaliator nothing; the punishment that works is the CUTOFF (denying 4 a turn for the rest of the match, against the 12 a betrayer gains), with the strike as the visible signal. **Kingslayer is retired**, and the reason is recorded in the module docstring and pinned by a test so it is not rebuilt: courting the leader needs help from the one player least likely to give it, so it struck 0.3 times per 35-move match and scored by hoarding instead. Raising the bonus 6 → 14 left it on 0.60 round wins either way, and the best rebuild scored 0.07 against a plain cooperator's 0.49. **Buzzer-Beater became Turncoat** — its calendar identity was the thing that stopped being true, so it is now the serial opportunist (buy trust, spend it, move to someone you have not burned), which is the seat that tests whether a 14 bonus is too strong. **Two presets now answer a betrayal with a strike, three with a cutoff, and Loyal Partner deliberately with neither** — it is kept as the pacifist control, so if it gets farmed while the rest hold up, that tells us the retaliation rule is what carries the field. Sandbagger is the one preset that must NOT strike back early, because that would spend the clean record it exists to save. Roster is seven: Tit-for-Tat, Loyal Partner, Turncoat, Dealmaker, Underdog's Champion, Sandbagger, Hoarder. Existing agents are unaffected — preset text is copied into an agent at creation. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1915 tests**.

- **The pot drops to 8 and the betrayal bonus rises to 14 — rules v8** (2026-08-19, branch `hoard-pot-8`, Direct Path) — two payoff constants move, derived rather than guessed. **The method:** a simulator was written and byte-checked against the real engine (399 random 8-player turns, identical results including pot splits, betrayals, mutual pacts and the score floor), then validated against the live v7 match data (simulated hoard rate 27% vs measured 25%, tie rate 23% vs ~19%). Everything below is measured on that, not reasoned in the abstract. **The pot 12 -> 8:** at 12, hoarding on your own partner beat sustaining a pact *at every horizon*, so a preset that simply sat in the pot all match won 62% of matches without ever using its own strategy. At 8 the ladder is 1.33 / 0.67 / 0.33 of a normal turn and cooperation wins until the last few turns. **The bonus 6 -> 14:** the knife was dead because `BETRAYAL_BONUS` (6) was *below* `HOARD_POT_POINTS` (8) — when a partner HELPed you, hoarding on them paid 12 and stabbing them paid 10, so hoarding was always the better way to defect. Sizing came from what a betrayal DESTROYS: the victim never partners with you again, so it has to cover several turns of lost pact income. At 14 a betrayal turn pays 18 = three normal turns, and stays under ~0.75 of a round so one knife cannot win a round outright. **`HURT_POINTS` deliberately stays at 8.** Raising it was tested and rejected: at 12 two-thirds of turns became a pile-on and winning scores halved (34.7 -> 18.0); at 16 rounds were won with 7 points and half were ties. The reason is structural — betrayal needs a willing victim so it is naturally rate-limited, while denial has no precondition and can mob. Denial stays break-even *on purpose*; it is the only brake in the system. **Six tests were restating payoffs instead of deriving them** and are now fixed at the root: `test_resolver` (four assertions), `test_inround_mirror` (three), and `test_viewer` (the rendered `+6 betrayal` chip). Several of their docstrings were stale from **v6** — still describing a +4 bonus and a -4 hurt — as were comments in `viewer.py` and `plan_rules.py`. `test_betrayer_floors_on_summed_delta` silently stopped exercising the score floor at the new bonus (two attackers no longer drove the delta negative); it now takes three and carries a guard assertion that pins the premise. **Mutation-verified:** setting the bonus back to 6 leaves all 1,915 tests green, which is the goal — nothing restates the number, so it is genuinely one constant to tune. `RULES_VERSION` v7 -> v8, per its own comment that it tracks the payoff table, and matching the v5 -> v6 precedent for a pure payoff-value change; without it, 12-point and 8-point matches are indistinguishable in the measurement data. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1915 tests**.

- **HOARD became a contested pot, and all eight presets were rewritten for it — rules v7** (2026-08-18, branches `hoard-pot` + `preset-rewrite-v7`, Direct Path) — the pot shipped in [#703](https://github.com/chrislawcodes/hoard-hurt-help/pull/703); this entry covers both it and the preset pass, since #703 went in without one. **Why:** twelve matches measured that day (nine at v5, three at v6) all ended the same way — the analyzer flagged HURT a **DEAD ACTION** in every single one, at 0-2%, and one v6 match had **zero attacks in 280 moves**. Sharpening the knife in [#702](https://github.com/chrislawcodes/hoard-hurt-help/pull/702) did not move it. **The root cause was that the game had never had a Prisoner's Dilemma defect move at all:** hoarding while your partner HELPed you paid 2 + 4 = 6, and a mutual pact paid 6, so cooperating and defecting were worth exactly the same and there was nothing for trust to be tested against. **The change:** everyone who HOARDs on a turn splits `HOARD_POT_POINTS` (12) between them, rounded down — alone you take 12, two of you 6 each, six of you 2 each. Slipping to the pot while a partner still HELPs you pays **16 against the pact's 6**, and a crowded pot pays less than cooperating, so greed self-limits. It also starves the runaway leader that decided most of the day's matches: the spare helper propping them up earned 0 and now has a 12-point alternative every turn. `hoard_share(n)` is the single source — resolver, viewer mirror, compact chip, per-move feed delta, replay legend and the agent-facing rules text all call it. Two display paths needed real work rather than a constant swap: `move_effect` cannot express a value that depends on the whole turn, so it reports the solo rate and `build_replay_view` overrides it with the turn's actual share. **`HOARD_POT_POINTS` is genuinely one number to tune** — verified by setting it to 24 and re-running the suite green, because every assertion derives from `hoard_share` rather than restating a payout. **The preset pass was not cosmetic.** Every preset was written for a game where HELP was the only move worth making, and none of them knew the pot existed — shipping v7 without this would have measured as no change at all, exactly the way v6 did. All eight were rewritten and cut from 4,209 characters to **2,205**. Four now reach for the pot; **Salvager was replaced by Hoarder**, the only preset whose default move is not HELP, because a mechanic nobody reaches for cannot be tested. Kingslayer was rebuilt after the data showed it was a **net donor of 16 points to the leader it meant to stop** — partnering the leader hands them the mutual bonus every turn, and it scored **0.00 round wins across all nine v5 matches** as a result; it now strikes the first turn the leader HELPs it. **The guard clause is gone from every preset** — "only HURT when it wins you the round" appeared in four of them, never evaluates true once anyone edges ahead, and is the measured reason betrayal died; a test now asserts it cannot come back. **Presets no longer quote any payoff figure at all.** Every number in them moved twice in one day (v6 and v7), so the rules text now owns the numbers and the presets own the behaviour, pinned by a guard that rejects any digit in a preset body and is mutation-verified. Full Preflight green both branches: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1915 tests**.

- **Betrayal payoffs sharpened so the knife is worth using — rules v6** (2026-08-18, branch `sharper-betrayal-payoffs`, Direct Path) — `BETRAYAL_BONUS` 4 → 6 and `HURT_POINTS` 4 → 8, driven by measurement rather than taste. **Ten real matches that day** (M_6598 / M_6602 / M_6604 / M_6606 / M_6607 / M_6609 / M_6611 / M_6613 / M_6615; M_6617 excluded at 32% defaults) recorded **22 HURTs in 2,511 moves — 0.9%**, a quarter of the 3.7% the pre-#701 roster managed, and M_6609 had **zero attacks in 280 moves**. The `game-design` analyzer on M_6602 flagged HURT a **DEAD ACTION**, three locked alliances, an average settle turn of **1.3 out of 5**, and a comeback **mathematically impossible in 7 of 7 rounds**. **The cause was the payoff table, not the prompts** — verified, not assumed: in M_6598 Kingslayer struck at R3T5 and R4T5 (a round's last turn) and Sandbagger at R6T5/R7T4/R7T5 (the final two rounds), exactly as their preset text instructs, so the strategies do reach the models and are followed. Betraying a helper paid the attacker 8 against a pact's 6 — a 2-point edge bought by losing the partner feeding you 6 a turn for the rest of the match. In an 8-player field that is a murder-suicide: attacker and victim both drop while six untouched players sail past, so every preset carrying "only HURT when it wins you the round" was reading the table correctly and standing down. At v6 a knife nets the attacker **10 against a pact's 6** and swings **18 points** between the two players in one turn (attacker 6 → 10, victim 6 → −8). **The four-outcome shape stays a genuine Prisoner's Dilemma** — Temptation 10 > Reward 6 > Punishment 2 > Sucker −8, and mutual cooperation still beats trading exploitation (6 > 1) — which is why this lever was chosen over a bash-the-leader bonus that would have pulled the game toward Diplomacy. `RULES_VERSION` v5 → v6, per its own comment that it tracks the payoff table. **A real bug surfaced and is fixed in the same commit:** the Compact view built its delta chips from literal strings (`"-4"`, `"+4"`, `"+2"`), so the engine would have paid −8 while the viewer showed −4 — the same rule living in two places. All three now derive from the constants, guarded by `test_compact_chips_match_the_payoff_constants` and **mutation-verified** (putting the literal back fails it). **Two knock-on corrections rather than stale reasoning left in place:** `strategy.py`'s rationale block said an extra helper (4) beat the knife (4), which v6 inverts (knife 6), and it rejected coercion because refusing paid 6 − 4 = 2 against complying's 0 — at `HURT_POINTS` = 8 refusing pays −2, so the threat now bites; recorded as an **open** route, not a covered one, since no preset bids for it. The Kingslayer preset's quoted swing moved twelve → eighteen and `test_per_game_strategy` pins it. **Not simulated, at Chris's direction** — this ships to be measured in real matches. **Known limitation, stated before building:** without a cap on incoming HELP a leader with a spare donor still banks 10 a turn (a pact's 6 plus a one-way 4), which is what decided M_6602, so this makes level rounds bite but does not by itself catch a runaway. Full Preflight green: ruff + mypy (212 files) + **1911 tests**.

- **The second rules-text builder is gone; there is now exactly one** (2026-08-17, branch `drop-dead-rules-text`, Direct Path) — cleanup finishing what [#687](https://github.com/chrislawcodes/hoard-hurt-help/pull/687) started. That PR dropped the `static.rules` key from the agent turn payload because the rulebook was being sent twice per turn, once as `rules` and again inside `base_prompt`. Deleted: `GameModule.rules_text` and `rules_text_for_match` (both the `Protocol` declaration and the `BaseGameModule` default), both games' overrides, both games' `make_rules_text`, and the `RULES_TEXT` module constant. What survives is the one text every agent-facing surface already read from — `semantic_rules_text` / `semantic_rules_text_for_match` / `make_game_rules_text` / `GAME_RULES_TEXT` — which `agent_base_prompt` embeds and MCP `get_instructions` serves. **Verified behaviour-neutral, not assumed:** the semantic block, the per-match base prompt, and the bare base prompt were hashed for **both** games on this branch and on `main`; all six SHA-256s match byte-for-byte, so nothing an agent receives moved. **No coverage was dropped — every test naming a removed symbol was rewritten onto the survivor, and two got stronger in the process.** `test_end_to_end`'s static-prefix test asserted `RULES_TEXT is snapshot` immediately after `snapshot = RULES_TEXT`, which could not fail under any change; it now builds the prefix the serving path actually sends and pins that two builds come out byte-identical (this is what prompt caching keys on). `test_mutual_help_modes` checked the mode→text mapping through `rules_text_for_match`, which after the removal would have duplicated the `semantic_rules_text_for_match` test sitting directly above it; it now covers the **count-based** entry point instead — the no-Match-row path the public `/agent-instructions` page renders, which nothing else in that file reached. `test_rules_single_source` dropped to the two surfaces that remain and its docstring no longer describes the deleted `static.rules` key. Both rewrites mutation-verified: making the base prompt vary between builds fails the first, and dropping the mode argument on the count-based path fails the second. Two test stub games implemented `rules_text` and now implement `semantic_rules_text`. Docs fixed too — [docs/writing-a-game-module.md](docs/writing-a-game-module.md) told anyone adding a game to implement a hook nothing would have called. **The removal exposed a fail-quiet, and it is fixed in the same PR.** `BaseGameModule.semantic_rules_text` returned `""` when a game didn't override it. Deleting `rules_text` moved the empty string onto the only remaining rules path: a future game module that forgot its rules would have served a **blank rulebook** over MCP and embedded a blank one in `base_prompt`, with nothing raising, nothing logging, and the agent simply told nothing about the game it was playing. The blast radius turned out to be zero — the full suite passed unchanged the moment the raise went in, so **no** stub or module ever relied on that empty string; it was pure latent risk, never exercised. New guard in [tests/test_rules_single_source.py](tests/test_rules_single_source.py) builds a rules-free `BaseGameModule` subclass and asserts both the bare and `_for_match` entry points raise; mutation-verified by putting `return ""` back (fails).
