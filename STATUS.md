## Recently Shipped

- **A sequential game's turn now goes only to the seat on the clock** (2026-08-14, branch `fix-sequential-serving-gate`) — turn serving decided who owed a move by asking one question: "has this seat submitted yet?" That is right for PD, where every seat acts every turn. It is wrong for Liar's Dice, where the module names one active actor and `validate_move` answers everyone else NOT_YOUR_TURN. **Two failures, and the second is the expensive one.** A seat that could not act was still handed a full turn payload, paid for a model think, and got a 400 back — and since a rejected move records nothing, the very next poll served it again, a paid loop with no exit. Worse: `select_next_turn` breaks an all-else-equal tie by agent id, so a user holding two seats in one match could have the *idle* seat win the tie every poll while the seat that actually owed the move was never served at all, and its turn ran out the clock and defaulted. The fix gates candidate collection on `next_actor` ([app/engine/agent_play_next_turn.py](app/engine/agent_play_next_turn.py)) — deliberately the same hook `SequentialDriver` already uses to pick whose turn to open and whose submission to wait for, so serving and the turn loop cannot disagree about whose move it is. Simultaneous games never reach it: `next_actor` is fail-loud in `BaseGameModule`, so consulting it on the PD path would 500 every PD poll, and a test pins that boundary. **Both bug tests were written first and watched fail against unmodified main**, then pass. The audit that found this also cleared four other candidates as *not* bugs (two duplicated guards that behave identically, a fork rule computed the same way in two places, and four behaviour-neutral micro-refactors). Follow-up flagged, not done: an idle sequential seat now costs 3 extra reads per long-poll tick (5s apart) — fine while Liar's Dice is admin-only and capped at 6 seats.
- **Session cookie lifetime is now a decision, not a default** (2026-08-14, branch `session-cookie-90-days`) — `SessionMiddleware` was created with no `max_age` ([app/main.py](app/main.py)), so the cookie lived for **Starlette's default 14 days**. Nobody had ever chosen that number; the privacy page simply documented the accident. Now an explicit `SESSION_MAX_AGE_SECONDS = 90 days`. **One number governs two things, and they are not separable.** The first-touch arrival record rides inside the session cookie (`app/identity/first_touch.py` writes to `request.session`), so the attribution window and how long someone stays signed in are the same value. Splitting them means giving arrival its own cookie — considered, and rejected as over-engineering for an Alpha, with the added cost of a second cookie to disclose on a privacy page we had just spent three passes shortening. **Why longer:** at 14 days someone who reads a Reddit post, bookmarks the site and returns three weeks later is recorded as `direct` — precisely the question `/admin/engagement` exists to answer. Usual attribution windows run 30–90 days; Chris picked the generous end. The other half of the trade is a login lasting a quarter, which is a feature on a site people check back on, and the blast radius of a stolen cookie is small here (no payments, no card). **Two tests, both verified to fail against the bug they guard** (each mutation run and reverted, not assumed): dropping the `max_age` argument reverts the wire value to `1209600` and is caught on the `Set-Cookie` header rather than by reading the constant back — reading the constant would still pass; and a page that *claims* a lifetime is checked against the real one, with N derived from the constant. **That second test inverted during the rebase**: it originally required the privacy page to state the lifetime, but the page was cut to its CalOPPA floor while this branch was open and no longer mentions one — nothing requires it to. The risk worth guarding is *wrong* information, not missing information, so it now passes when the page is silent and arms itself the moment the sentence returns; verified by injecting a stale "14 days" (fails) and a correct "90 days" (passes). A third pins the value to whole days, so the day-count comparison cannot go quietly unfalsifiable at, say, 90.5. That second test is the one that matters historically: this page has already shipped a sentence that was true when written and false a week later, and the fix then was a test added after the fact. **Worth knowing:** `FIRST_TOUCH_CAPTURE_ENABLED` still defaults to false and is the deploy gate for capture — if it is off in Railway, nothing is being recorded and the window length is moot until it is flipped. Tests: `tests/test_session_cookie_lifetime.py` (+3).

- **Terms cut to 311 words, section by section** (2026-08-14, branch `terms-rewrite`) — the companion to the privacy trim, worked through with Chris one section at a time. **611 words → 311**, nine sections → six. **The cutting rule is the opposite of the privacy page's, and that distinction drove every call:** everything on a privacy policy is a disclosure the reader is owed, so cutting reduces obligations; most of the terms are *rights and shields*, so cutting reduces protection. The rule became **keep rights and shields, cut descriptions and specific rules** — which is why the page reads shorter while claiming *more*. **Fair Play was deleted entirely.** Listing seven banned behaviours narrowed us: anything unlisted was arguably allowed. "We can suspend or delete any account at any time, for any reason" is the real enforcement power and covers all of them — so the Accounts line now carries that whole section's weight and must not be softened. Chris also rejected "your agent's words and actions are yours" as unfair, since a model generates the words; the principle survives implicitly in "everything done under your account is your responsibility", which was left alone deliberately rather than carved out. **The licence gained reach while losing words.** "For any purpose" replaced a 20-word list of purposes (listing purposes limits you to them). "Can't take that permission back" replaced six words of the same idea and is the highest-value clause on the page: a licence silent on revocability is generally revocable at will, match history is *shared* so one player withdrawing damages everyone else's record, and published work cannot be unpublished. **"To pass those rights on to others" was added late**, when Chris raised selling results — publishing a free dataset needs only our own publish right, but a *buyer* needs rights we cannot grant without it. **Two contradictions were caught and fixed rather than shipped.** The privacy page's "we do not sell your data" collided with a licence permitting sale of match data; narrowed to "personal information", with an explicit acquisition-transfer clause. And **selling the mailing list turns out to be barred upstream** — Google's API Services User Data Policy forbids transferring Google-derived user data to third parties even aggregated, and the penalty is a revoked OAuth app, i.e. sign-in breaks for everyone. Ad trackers on our own pages stay fine; piping user emails to an ad platform does not. **The warranty and liability section was deliberately NOT plain-Englished** — "as is", "as available" and the indirect/incidental/consequential triad are terms of art, and a disclaimer can be struck for not being clear and prominent. It is now a third of the page: everything else got cut hard, the shield did not. Note it is also the *only* remaining place AI costs are excluded, after the AI section was folded into Using the site. Tests: two repointed (the licence test moved off naming strategies and onto the scope that covers them — "anything you put here", "for any purpose"), one deleted with the sentence it guarded, one added pinning the irrevocable and pass-on clauses; the new one mutation-verified against both removals.

- **Engagement dashboard, and the records that make it possible** (2026-08-13, branch `admin-engagement-dashboard`) — there was no way to answer either "where do new users fall out on the way to playing?" or "which traffic source produced someone who actually played?". Nothing in the app recorded a traffic source at all: no analytics script, no referrer capture, no UTM handling anywhere in the codebase. The engagement half was worse in a subtler way — the data existed and nobody had ever read it. New admin-only `/admin/engagement`, in both admin nav surfaces. **The first two designs were wrong and the reviews killed them.** A strict funnel (count someone at step N only if they cleared 1..N-1) cannot work here, for two reasons that only turned up under attack. **There is no single path — there are at least four:** AI agent set up through the web; AI agent connected through MCP, which stamps a live connection before the user has an agent *or a handle*; **manual human play, which never reaches "AI connected" at all and is the DEFAULT for a brand-new user** (`web_join._default_human_choice` returns True for anyone with no history, and its own comment calls it "the no-setup path"); and platform bots. Under strict nesting a person playing by hand every day rendered as *stuck at picked a handle*. **And the app deletes the evidence a drop-off report needs** — five confirmed sites: incomplete connection setups are garbage collected after 24h, held seats are deleted within ~15 minutes (`seat_hold.py:87`), agents with no game history are hard-*deleted* rather than archived (`agents_lifecycle.py:158`), admin match delete removes players and turns, and a handle reset nulls the handle columns. So the answer is a durable append-only `user_milestones` table written when each milestone happens, presented as **independent counts, not a waterfall**. **The recording mechanism took three attempts, all refuted by running them, all failing silently.** `after_commit` cannot emit SQL (raises, caller's commit still reports OK, **0 rows** — and the advisory error handler swallowed it). `db.begin_nested()` is a **no-op inside a flush**, so the savepoint protected nothing and a duplicate would have raised out of the caller's commit. And `after_insert` is a **mapper** event: binding it to `Session` is accepted without raising and then fires **zero times** — the fix for the second bug reintroduced the first. Working shape: `after_insert` on the model classes collects into `session.info`, `after_flush_postexec` on `Session` writes via a Core insert inside a connection-level savepoint, `IntegrityError` caught *inside* the savepoint as "already recorded". **Filtering bots by agent kind is nowhere near enough** — in the dev database the house, sim and harness accounts own **over 500 of 646 player rows** and all but one run agents marked as real AI. A stored `users.is_internal` flag does the work, set at creation and never recomputed, because both things you would key on instead change afterwards (`sync_google_user` rewrites the email on a later login; promote/demote changes the role). Against a copy of the dev DB the backfill flags **12 of 13 accounts** as ours. **Source capture ships behind `FIRST_TOUCH_CAPTURE_ENABLED`, default false, and that flag is the deploy gate** — merging auto-deploys (`railway.json` `preDeployCommand`), so "decide before production" and "merge the PR" are the same instant; the flag is what makes the decision separable. Turning it on writes a session cookie for anonymous visitors, which the site does not do today, on a site with **no privacy policy or cookie notice anywhere**. **Deliberately not done:** no third-party analytics (so no visitor count — the page says so); no privacy notice (Chris's call, and the flag defaults off until there is one); no event log, so admin deletes still make a past week non-reproducible. Two read-only preview scripts (`scripts/preview_internal_backfill.py`, `scripts/preview_milestone_backfill.py`) load the migrations' own queries so a rehearsal cannot drift from what runs. Tests: +50 across 7 files, several verified to FAIL against a broken implementation before being kept — the registration guard took three attempts, because a model registers itself when its own module is imported, so the obvious schema assertion passes even with the export deleted. Unblocked: "did r/hermesagent actually work?" becomes answerable, once the flag is turned on.
